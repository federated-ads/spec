# Code of Conduct

The Federated Ads Protocol project adopts the [W3C Code of Conduct](https://www.w3.org/policies/code-of-conduct/). This page summarises how it applies here and how to report a problem. Where this page and the W3C Code of Conduct differ, the W3C Code of Conduct takes precedence.

## Our commitment

We want this project to be a welcoming place for publishers, advertisers, engineers, researchers, civil-society advocates and anyone else with something to contribute, whatever their background, identity, experience or employer.

## Expected behaviour

- Be respectful and considerate, including when you disagree.
- Criticise ideas, not people. Strong technical disagreement is welcome.
- Assume good faith, and ask before assuming the worst.
- Make room for others, especially people new to the project or to standards work.
- Disclose relevant affiliations and conflicts of interest when they bear on a discussion.
- Disclose substantial AI assistance in your contributions, as [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) asks.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, intimidation or threats, in public or in private.
- Discriminatory, demeaning or sexualised language or imagery.
- Personal attacks, insults and deliberate disruption.
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission.
- Sharing competitively sensitive commercial information in project channels (see the competition-law note in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md)).

## Scope

This applies in all project spaces: GitHub issues, discussions and pull requests, mailing lists, calls, meetings and events. It also applies when you represent the project elsewhere.

## Reporting

Report concerns to the project lead, Suneesh Rajan, at suneeshtr@gmail.com. Reports are kept confidential and shared only as needed to resolve them. If the report concerns the project lead, or you would prefer to report to someone independent, you can contact the [W3C Ombudspeople](https://www.w3.org/guide/process/coc-incident-resolution-ombuds.html) once the work is hosted in a W3C Community Group.

Please include what happened, where and when, who was involved, and any links or screenshots. You do not need to have every detail before reporting.

## Enforcement

The project lead, and later the editors or chairs, will review each report promptly and fairly. Depending on what happened, responses range from a private conversation or a public correction to a temporary or permanent ban from project spaces. People who report in good faith will not face retaliation.

The project is small and currently run by one person. As more editors join, enforcement will move to a group of at least two people, and nobody will handle a report in which they are involved.
